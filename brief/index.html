Project brief

Why it is built this way.

Before designing anything I read through oiltaxpro.com properly, including the suitability page. Almost every decision in this demo traces back to something already stated there.

This page lists those decisions, what the build covers, and what going live would actually involve.

Decisions

Six choices, and what drove each one.

Each quote below is your own copy. Beside it is what the demo does about it.

“A flashy pitch in your inbox, complete with a 24-hour countdown. There’s always a webinar, too, promising all the secrets of the rich.”

oiltaxpro.com

No countdown, no seat counter, no manufactured scarcity

There is no timer anywhere in the build and no invented number of remaining places. The only deadline referenced is the year-end calendar, which is real, and it is explained as a mechanism instead of used as pressure.

“This Is Not a Fit If: You are seeking guarantees, projections, or specific outcomes.”

oiltaxpro.com

No dollar figures and no projections

No headline promises a saving. The page sells the explanation, not an outcome. Every reference to tax treatment is written as conditional on structure, timing and the individual, which is both accurate and consistent with how you already talk.

“Not Everyone Qualifies, And That’s By Design.”

oiltaxpro.com

Qualification sits inside the registration

Registration captures an accreditation attestation and a separate consent before anything else. Your fit and not-a-fit criteria appear on the page close to verbatim, so people rule themselves out before taking a seat instead of on your call.

“Nobody really tells you where those numbers come from... No one talks about what happens if the timing falls apart.”

oiltaxpro.com

Risk is a module, not fine print

Section four of the webinar is the risks, stated directly, and the page says that section is the reason the session exists. Dry holes, price exposure, illiquidity, operator quality and capital calls are all named instead of softened.

“Designed to sit next to your CPA, not replace them.”

oiltaxpro.com

The CPA is an ally in the copy, never a competitor

The tax section and the deck both frame the session as giving attendees better questions to bring their own adviser. That lowers the defensiveness of anyone who already has one, which is most of your audience.

Current funnel: ads, contact form, VSL, then a suitability call.

From your note

The webinar is additive and replaces nothing

Your campaigns and your booking process stay as they are. The webinar sits between initial interest and the suitability call, so people arrive at that call already understanding the structures and the risks. The funnel diagram on the landing page marks which steps are existing and which are new.

What is built

Three working pieces, not a mockup.

Everything below is live and clickable. Registration validates and confirms, the deck runs with presenter notes, and the automation flow switches by attendance path.

Part one Landing page

Registration with accreditation attestation and consent, session selection, confirmation state, the educational sections and the funnel fit diagram.

Open → Part two Webinar deck

Twenty-two slides covering structures, where deductions come from, timing, risk and a diligence framework. Presenter notes on every slide, press N.

Open → Part three Automation sequence

Draft messages for before and after the session, branching on whether someone attended, left early, missed it or booked. Includes the suppression rules.

Going live

What it would take from here.

What I would do

01Connect registration to your webinar platform and push registrants into the CRM you already use, with the attestation and consent stored against each record.
02Build the sequence in your existing email tool, wired to attendance data so the branches fire correctly, with booking suppression as the first rule.
03Swap the placeholders for your own footage and photography, and set the real session schedule.
04Move it to your domain, enable indexing, and hand over the files so nothing is locked to me.

What I would need from you

01Tool access or names. Which webinar platform, CRM and email tool you run, so the integration is scoped against what exists instead of guessed.
02A headshot and session footage. The portrait in the demo is a low-resolution reference, and the hero footage is a placeholder visualization, not a recording.
03Format and dates. Whether you want live, evergreen, or both. The build supports either.
04Your approval on copy. I have written everything conservatively, with no projections or guarantees, but the disclosures and claims should be reviewed on your side before anything runs.
