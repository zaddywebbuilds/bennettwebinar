Understanding the opportunity

Your capital is attached to a physical asset.

A non-operated working interest is a direct interest in a specific well, drilled by a specific operator, on a specific pad. Its schedule is set by permits, rigs and crews, not by markets.

That is why the tax treatment exists, and it is also why the risk is real. Everything in the session follows from it.

01Real operations. Permitting, drilling, completion and production each take time and capital, and each carries its own way of going wrong.
02Real operators. Who drills the well is the largest single variable in the outcome, usually larger than the geology.
03Real risk. Wells can underperform or fail to produce commercially. Capital committed is capital at risk of total loss.

Tax considerations

How oil & gas investments affect your taxes

Understanding the mechanisms, and the conditions attached to each, is what lets you judge whether any of this is relevant to your own position.

Educational overviewTax treatment depends on your individual circumstances and the specific investment structure. Nothing here is tax advice.
01Intangible drilling costs

Intangible drilling costs

Spend tied to the drilling work itself, which leaves nothing behind to sell: labor, fuel, drilling fluid and site preparation.

Rig operations and laborFuel and utilitiesDrilling fluid and materialsSite preparation

Nothing in this category can be recovered and resold once the work is done. Policy has long treated it differently from equipment, because encouraging domestic drilling was a deliberate choice rather than an accident.

02Tangible drilling costs

Tangible drilling costs

Physical assets that remain with the well: casing, wellhead, surface piping and production equipment. Recovered over time.

Casing and tubingWellhead componentsSurface equipmentProduction equipment

These assets still exist after the work is finished, so the cost is recovered over time instead of in one period. This is the category most often confused with the first.

03Depletion

Depletion

An allowance recognizing that a reservoir is a finite resource being consumed as it produces. It follows production, not spend.

Surface facilityWellboreReservoir rockHydrocarbon zone

This one follows actual production rather than spend, so it depends on what the well does after it is drilled, not on what was committed before.

How oil & gas wells work

One well, from surface to lateral.

A working interest follows the physical work. Understanding the sequence below is what lets you judge where costs arise, when timing matters, and what is actually at risk at each stage.

Surface operations

The rig, power units, tanks, pipe racks and crew facilities. This is where drilling is powered and controlled, and where most of the visible capital sits during the drilling phase.

Vertical wellbore

The bore is drilled straight down through shallow formations. Successive casing strings are run and cemented in place to isolate groundwater and stabilize the hole before drilling continues.

Build section

Over a measured radius the bore turns from vertical to horizontal, steered to arrive inside the target interval at the correct depth and direction.

Horizontal lateral

The bore runs within the target formation, often for thousands of feet. Exposing far more reservoir rock than a vertical well is why wells are drilled this way. It is later perforated and stimulated so hydrocarbons can flow in.

Geological formations

Layered sedimentary rock laid down over geological time. The darker interval the lateral follows is the organic-rich target; the layers above it act as seals that keep hydrocarbons contained.

Build & lateral

The bore curves from vertical to horizontal and is drilled along the target formation, often for thousands of feet.

The bulk of drilling spend typically sits here
Stage 04

Completion

Production casing is set, the lateral is perforated, and the formation is stimulated so hydrocarbons can flow into the wellbore.

Placed-in-service timing is decided around here
Stage 05

Production & decline

The well flows, with output highest early and declining over time. Revenue, operating costs and any depletion allowance follow actual production.

Questions

Before you register.

Who is this session for?

Accredited investors and high-income professionals who consistently pay substantial federal tax and want to understand how oil and gas tax structures work before deciding whether they are relevant. It is educational, not an offer.

Does attending commit me to anything?

No. Nothing is offered or sold on the session, and there is no obligation of any kind. Attendees who want to go further can request a suitability conversation afterwards; most will not, and that is a legitimate outcome.

Are oil and gas investments suitable for everyone?

No. All investing carries risk, and these are private, illiquid, long-horizon investments that are unsuitable for many people regardless of income. Potential tax treatment depends on the specific structure and on your individual circumstances. The session covers these limits directly.

Will specific returns or tax savings be presented?

No. No projections, guaranteed savings, or performance figures are presented. The session explains how structures are designed to work and what determines whether a benefit applies at all.

Is it live or recorded?

As proposed here, each session runs live and includes live Q&A, and everyone registered receives the replay whether or not they attend. An evergreen on-demand format is equally supported if preferred.

Why has my CPA never raised this?

Most CPAs are engaged to report accurately on what already happened rather than to explore alternative structures ahead of time. Oil and gas sits where tax law, energy markets, and private investing overlap, which falls outside many practices’ scope.

What happens after the session?

You receive the replay and a short summary. If you want to explore whether the strategy applies to your situation, you can request a one-to-one suitability call. There is no follow-up pressure either way.
